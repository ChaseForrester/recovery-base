The complete guide to ice bath recovery

Cold water has a long history with athletes and with people who simply want to feel less inflamed and more steady. At Recovery Base you can use it in a communal bath or a private one.

What the cold does

In cold water, blood vessels constrict and circulation shifts toward your core. When you step out and warm up, circulation increases again, bringing oxygen and nutrients back to muscle. People use that cycle for soreness, joints, and a general sense of reset.

  • Less inflammation and soreness
  • Support for muscle repair
  • A lift in alertness from endorphins and norepinephrine
  • Practice at staying calm when the body wants to tense

How long

A useful starting range is 8–12°C. Beginners often stay for 2 to 5 minutes. Experienced plungers may stay longer, up to about 10 minutes. You don’t need to endure it. Steady breathing matters more than the clock.

How often

For most people, two or three sessions a week is plenty. After a heavy training block, cold can help the body settle. On lighter weeks, once is enough to keep the habit. If you’ve never plunged, a guide will walk you through it — you won’t be left guessing.
