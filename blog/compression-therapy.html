The complete guide to compression therapy

Recovery should be as intentional as training. Compression therapy is a simple way to reduce soreness, boost circulation, and support your body’s own recovery process.

What it is

Compression therapy uses air-filled boots or sleeves to apply controlled pressure, usually to the legs. In the compression lounge, professional-grade boots gently squeeze and release in rhythmic waves — similar to a deep massage, with consistent pressure.

  • Increase circulation
  • Help move lactic acid and waste
  • Reduce muscle fatigue and swelling
  • Support recovery after training

Why it helps

Training creates tiny microtears. That’s part of getting stronger, and it can leave you sore. Better blood flow delivers nutrients to those tissues and helps move metabolic waste, which means less inflammation and an easier return to movement. If you’re on your feet all day, the same circulation makes a noticeable difference by evening.

What a session feels like

Chambers in the sleeves inflate and deflate in sequence, a wave moving up the limb. That mimics the way circulation already works, helping push fluid back toward the heart. When the pressure releases, fresher blood flows back in. Athletes use it between sessions. Desk days benefit just as much.
